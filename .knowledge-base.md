## Source summaries

### Semilocal Sonin spaces

Connes, Consani and Moscovici, *Zeta zeros and prolate wave operators*, arXiv:2310.18423v2. [Primary text](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.18423v2).

Definition 4.5 imposes simultaneous spatial and Fourier gaps below \(\lambda\). Section 4.5 uses shell indicators:

\[
\epsilon_n=\mathbf1_{\{|x|_p=p^n\}},\qquad \sigma_p=\epsilon_0-p^{-1}\epsilon_1.
\]

The Fourier-fixed \(\sigma_p\) defines \(\theta_S f=[(\bigotimes_{p\in S_f}\sigma_p)\otimes f]\). Equation (57) gives multiplier \(\prod_{p\in S_f}(1-p^{-1/2-it})\). Section 4.7 proves Fourier intertwining and \(\langle\theta_S f,\eta_S g\rangle=\langle f,g\rangle\), where \(\eta_S\) uses unit balls. Theorem 4.6 gives topological Sonin isomorphisms. Equations (51)-(53), (59)-(62) give a common entire carrier \(\mathcal B_\lambda\) with varying norms:

\[
q_S(F,G)=\int F(1/2+it)\overline{G(1/2+it)}|E_S(t)|^{-2}\,dt,
\quad E_S(t)=\prod_{v\in S}L_v(1/2+it).
\]

Some proposition numbers fail in HTML; cite sections and equations.

### Geometry and trace

Alain Connes, *The Riemann Hypothesis: Past, Present and a Letter Through Time*, arXiv:2602.04022v1, 3 February 2026. *Geometric Perspectives* is §7, not the paper title. [Primary text](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.04022v1).

Section 7.3, equations (23)-(24), defines \(Y_S=\mathbb A_S/\Gamma_S\), with \(\Gamma_S=\mathbb Z_S^\times\). Theorem 7.2 organizes \(\mathcal S(\mathbb A_S)\rtimes\Gamma_S\) as sections of a sheaf on \(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z\); its generic stalk is the global crossed product. Section 7.4 relates the semilocal trace formula to the Weil form, retaining the projections and modulus. The source describes the related function spaces through Hochschild homology. This supplies a geometric setting, not a proved primitive polarization or identification of the Sonin realization with such a polarized complex. Section 6.6 retains comparison and ground-state obligations. Equation (25) is missing from the web text extraction; workers needing its exact operator expression must inspect the PDF.

### Arithmetic divisors

Alain Connes and Caterina Consani, *On the Jacobian of \(\overline{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z}\)*, arXiv:2602.15941v1, 17 February 2026. [Primary text](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.15941v1).

Definition 3.1 uses rank-one torsion-free abelian groups with a possibly degenerate real seminorm. For \(a=(a_f,a_\infty)\), §3.4 defines \(L_a=\{q\in\mathbb Q:qa_f\in\widehat{\mathbb Z}\}\) and \(\|v\|_a=|a_\infty||v|\). Theorem 3.4 identifies their Picard monoid with \(\mathbb Q^\times\backslash\mathbb A/\widehat{\mathbb Z}^{\times}\). Theorem 8.15 gives the framed/rooted uniformizations; §8.5 already supplies the framed bijection. These constructions do not give a Hodge-index theorem for the Weil test form. A Picard monoid with singular metrics cannot be used as if it were a principally polarized abelian variety without further construction.

### Classical comparison

Alain Connes and Caterina Consani, *Weil positivity and Trace formula, the archimedean place*, arXiv:2006.13771v1, 24 June 2020. Appendix B, equations (147)-(150), fixes the full explicit formula and its archimedean subtraction. The multiplicative test corresponding to our additive \(h\) is \(x^{-1/2}h(\log x)\). Its Mellin transform is \(F_h(s-1/2)\). Finite places enter with a minus sign in the zero sum. The source's restricted archimedean positivity does not establish arbitrary semilocal positivity. [Appendix B](https://arxiv.org/html/2006.13771v1#A2).

Alain Connes, Caterina Consani and Matilde Marcolli, *The Weil proof and the geometry of the adeles class space*, arXiv:math/0703392v1, 13 March 2007. Sections 4.2-4.5 construct a restriction-map cokernel and descended trace pairing; §5 compares this with primitive cohomology. Equation (4.44) includes all zeros with multiplicity in a rapidly decreasing function-space realization. Theorem 6.1 is the trace formula. Proposition 6.2 makes positivity equivalent to the corresponding RH. Sections 7.1-7.2 supply scaling correspondences, degree terms and the limits of the finite-field dictionary. These statements do not prove the sign. [Primary text](https://arxiv.org/html/math/0703392v1).

### Earlier constructions

Alain Connes, *Trace formula in noncommutative geometry and the zeros of the Riemann zeta function*, arXiv:math/9811068v1. Theorem 4 in §VII proves the semilocal cutoff trace formula. It uses \(R_\Lambda=\widehat P_\Lambda P_\Lambda\), retains the divergent term \(2h(1)\log'\Lambda\), and normalizes the local principal values by additive Fourier transformation. Theorem 5 in §VIII gives an RH-equivalent global trace condition. Those are distinct statements. [Original source](https://arxiv.org/html/math/9811068v1).

Christopher Deninger, *Arithmetic Geometry and Analysis on Foliated Spaces*, arXiv:math/0505354. Section 3 derives the weight-one pairing identity from conjectural arithmetic cohomology and a positive Hodge-star pairing. Theorem 4.1 proves a corresponding weight identity for a compact three-manifold with a Riemannian surface foliation, dense leaf and compatible conformal flow. It does not construct such an arithmetic manifold. [Author's text](https://arxiv.org/html/math/0505354).

Jean-Francois Burnol, *Two complete and minimal systems associated with the zeros of the Riemann zeta function*, arXiv:math/0203120. The introduction and §2 connect co-Poisson summation to Sonin spaces and Gamma-completed Mellin transforms. This predates the present project and supplies the analytic background; no claim of inventing entire Sonin realizations is appropriate. [Original source](https://arxiv.org/html/math/0203120).

## Shared test algebra

Use \(\mathcal T=C_c^\infty(\mathbb R,\mathbb C)\),

\[
(f*g)(x)=\int f(u)g(x-u)\,du,\quad f^*(x)=\overline{f(-x)},
\quad F_f(z)=\int f(x)e^{zx}\,dx,
\quad\widehat f(t)=F_f(-it).
\]

Then \(F_{f*g}=F_fF_g\), \(F_{f^*}(z)=\overline{F_f(-\overline z)}\). The conventions are those of APP's `research/weil-conventions.md`, checked against Appendix B above. They use the full complex algebra, not only even or real tests.

For nontrivial zeros counted with multiplicity, set

\[
W(h)=\sum_\rho F_h(\rho-1/2),\qquad
B_W(f,g)=W(f*g^*).
\]

The quadratic summand is \(F_f(\rho-1/2)\overline{F_f(1/2-\overline\rho)}\). It becomes an absolute square only when \(\operatorname{Re}\rho=1/2\). Repeated integration by parts gives rapid decay in the critical strip; the unconditional zero-count estimate makes the sum absolutely convergent. Convergence alone supplies no sign.

Write \(W=P-A-\sum_pW_p\), where

\[
P(h)=F_h(1/2)+F_h(-1/2),\qquad
W_p(h)=\log p\sum_{k\ge1}p^{-k/2}\{h(k\log p)+h(-k\log p)\},
\]

\[
A(h)=(\log(4\pi)+\gamma)h(0)
+\int_0^\infty\frac{e^{-u/2}(h(u)+h(-u))-2e^{-u}h(0)}{1-e^{-2u}}\,du.
\]

This is the additive change of variables in Appendix B. The cancellation at zero must be retained. For \(h=f*g^*\), \(P(h)\) contains mixed moment products, not an unconditional sum of moment squares. Pole-killing tests satisfy \(F_f(1/2)=F_f(-1/2)=0\); working only there still requires a justified route back to all tests before invoking the full Weil criterion.

If \(\operatorname{supp} f,\operatorname{supp}g\subset[-a,a]\), only \(p^k\le e^{2a}\) contribute. Thus the finite formula agrees with the global distribution at support-admissible pairs \((S,a)\) containing all primes at most \(e^{2a}\). A new prime at the same admissible support has zero local contribution. Nonzero local comparisons concern intermediate place sets or enlarged support.

## Local derivations

The following elementary derivations use the explicit factor in the supplied perspective. They are not novelty claims or geometric intersection theorems.

Let \(L=\log p>0\), \(r=p^{-1/2}\), \(m_p(t)=1-re^{-itL}\), and \(T_Lu(x)=u(x-L)\). The operator \(A_p=I-rT_L\) on \(L^2(\mathbb R,dx)\) is bounded and invertible, with inverse \(\sum_{k\ge0}r^kT_L^k\) convergent in operator norm. Fourier transformation gives multiplier \(m_p\). Therefore

\[
(1-r)\|u\|_2\le\|A_pu\|_2\le(1+r)\|u\|_2,
\quad A_p^*A_p-I=r^2I-r(T_L+T_L^*).
\]

In a common weighted carrier this yields

\[
q_{S\cup\{p\}}(F,G)-q_S(F,G)
=\int F\overline G\,(r^2-2r\cos(tL))w_S(t)\,dt.
\]

The defect multiplier is negative near \(t=0\) and positive near \(t=\pi/L\). This gives both signs on the ambient weighted \(L^2\) space by compact frequency localization. A separate argument is needed to transfer either sign to a fixed Sonin subspace; that subspace is not invariant under arbitrary frequency localization.

For \(\sigma>0\), direct geometric expansion gives

\[
\partial_\sigma\log|1-p^{-\sigma-it}|^2
=2L\sum_{k\ge1}p^{-k\sigma}\cos(ktL).
\]

The series is uniformly absolutely convergent in \(t\) for fixed \(\sigma>0\). Since \(\widehat h\in L^1\), termwise integration and Fourier inversion give

\[
\frac1{2\pi}\int\widehat h(t)
\left.\partial_\sigma\log|1-p^{-\sigma-it}|^2\right|_{\sigma=1/2}\,dt=W_p(h).
\]

The logarithmic derivative produces all prime powers; the raw norm defect does not. If the intended arithmetic identity is \(B_W=-I\), then the required primitive intersection increment has sign \(+W_p\), while the Weil increment is \(-W_p\).

Multipliers for different primes commute. This proves the square for these bounded analytic maps. It does not construct a square of primitive geometric correspondences or control mixed intersection terms.

## Pointwise normalization obstruction

This is a proposed paper result with a complete elementary argument for the specific candidate. It must receive independent mathematical review before publication. No novelty is claimed.

Consider the carrier identity \(j:L^2(w_Sdt)\to L^2(w_{S\cup\{p\}}dt)\), with \(w_{S\cup\{p\}}=|m_p|^2w_S\). Its ambient polar isometry is \(U=j(j^*j)^{-1/2}\), hence

\[
(UF)(1/2+it)=\frac{F(1/2+it)}{|1-p^{-1/2-it}|}.
\]

There are no nonzero entire functions \(F,G\) satisfying this boundary relation. Indeed set

\[
D_p(z)=(1-p^{-z})(1-p^{z-1}).
\]

On the critical line, \(D_p(z)=|1-p^{-z}|^2>0\), so the relation implies \(D_pG^2=F^2\) there. Both sides are entire. The identity theorem extends equality to \(\mathbb C\), including when the initial equality holds almost everywhere: continuity makes it hold everywhere on that line. At \(z=0\),

\[
D_p(0)=0,\qquad D_p'(0)=\log p\,(1-p^{-1})\ne0.
\]

For nonzero entire \(F,G\), their orders at zero are finite integers. Equality requires \(1+2\operatorname{ord}_0G=2\operatorname{ord}_0F\), a contradiction. Thus pointwise polar normalization takes every nonzero entire carrier element outside the class of entire functions.

This rejects the ambient multiplier correction as a transition preserving \(\mathcal B_\lambda\). It does not reject the intrinsic polar decomposition of the bounded identity between two Hilbert norms on \(\mathcal B_\lambda\): its adjoint is obtained from the restricted forms, and is generally a compressed operator rather than multiplication by \(|m_p|^2\). Whether that intrinsic correction preserves the arithmetic test maps and trace comparison remains a different problem.

An unnormalized carrier identity can preserve entire functions but cannot be an ambient isometry because \(|m_p|^2\ne1\). A complex reciprocal multiplier can avoid the square root but introduces poles at \(z=2\pi ik/L\); cancellation requires an additional vanishing condition on the particular input.

## Local signs and orthogonal extensions

The local distribution can be tested exactly by disjoint translated bumps. Let a nonzero real \(\psi\in C_c^\infty\) have support in \((-\epsilon,\epsilon)\), with \(2\epsilon<L\), and take \(f=\sum_{j=0}^nc_j\psi(\cdot-jL)\). Then

\[
W_p(f*f^*)=L\|\psi\|_2^2\sum_{i\ne j}c_i\overline{c_j}r^{|i-j|}.
\]

The pole moments vanish if \(C(r)=C(r^{-1})=0\), where \(C(z)=\sum c_jz^j\). Thus choose \(C(z)=(1-(r+r^{-1})z+z^2)B(z)\). The Toeplitz symbol is

\[
a_r(\theta)=2\sum_{k\ge1}r^k\cos(k\theta)
=\frac{1-r^2}{1-2r\cos\theta+r^2}-1.
\]

It is positive at zero and negative at \(\pi\). The polynomial factor has no zero on the unit circle. An exact finite witness is simpler than a limiting argument. Put \(a=r+r^{-1}\). Direct expansion gives

\[
Q_r(1,-a,1)=-4-2r^2,\qquad
Q_r(1,1-a,1-a,1)=-8-4r^2+6r+2/r.
\]

At \(p=11\) these are respectively \(-46/11\) and \((28\sqrt{11}-92)/11>0\). The latter sign follows from \(49\cdot11>23^2\). Both coefficient polynomials are divisible by \(1-az+z^2\), so both pole moments vanish exactly. The smooth bump identity therefore proves both signs for an actual prime distribution on compact smooth tests. This derivation was independently checked by symbolic expansion; it awaits external review. The root agent's detailed working derivation is [local-sign-calculation.md](research/local-sign-calculation.md).

For a candidate with \(I_T\le0\), an isometric inclusion \(j\), and an orthogonal decomposition \(\Gamma_Tf=j\Gamma_Sf+d_pf\), the increment is \(I_T(d_pf,d_pf)\le0\). Exact comparison with \(+W_p(f*f^*)\) fails on a positive witness. This obstruction is specific to orthogonal nonpositive enlargement and to a nontrivial intermediate extension. It does not forbid correspondences with nonzero mixed terms, degree corrections or varying primitive projections.

Its corrected real symplectic form uses an imaginary part; antisymmetrizing a complex sesquilinear expression is not automatically a sesquilinear pairing.

The inspected `analytic-results.md` distinguishes strong convergence from operator-norm convergence. Proper zero-extended cutoffs retain a unit kernel vector and cannot converge in operator norm to a bounded-below operator. Uniform bounds plus stability on a dense linear core can instead construct a strong limit. These hypotheses must be proved for the actual adelic operators. Finite norm comparisons above give no uniform all-prime estimate.

For unitary dilation \(D_bf(x)=b^{1/2}f(bx)\), spatial and Fourier gaps become \(\lambda/b\) and \(b\lambda\). Thus the defining Sonin gap conditions do not supply invariance of a fixed \(\lambda\) under all dilations. A proposed generator needs a domain and an invariant realization before any self-adjointness claim. The adjoint identity \(\Theta^\dagger=1-\Theta\), with domain equality, would make \((\Theta-1/2)/i\) self-adjoint. The identity and full zero realization remain construction obligations.

It should distinguish the known harmonic-analytic map from an unconstructed geometric correspondence. Part II owns candidate obstructions, including the precise distinction between ambient and compressed normalization, the primitive local-sign test, and support-indexed compatibility. The final conjecture needs a specified complex \(P_{S,a}\), primitive projection, Hermitian pairing derived from arithmetic duality, test maps, prime transition maps and comparison equations including boundary terms. A nonpositive primitive sign and exact all-test identity \(B_W=-I(\Gamma\cdot,\Gamma\cdot)\) would imply RH by the classical Weil criterion. This is the desired package, not an available premise for claiming the arithmetic construction is solved.
