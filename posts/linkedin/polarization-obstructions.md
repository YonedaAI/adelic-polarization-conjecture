A local prime form can take both signs even after both pole moments vanish. Obstructions to Adelic Polarization gives exact smooth examples at p=11 and positive examples at every prime. This 21-page paper asks whether normalization or an orthogonal enlargement can supply a geometric pairing compatible with those arithmetic contributions.

One obstruction comes from complex analysis. Positive pointwise polar normalization would require an identity of entire functions in which an Euler denominator times a square equals another square. The denominator has a simple zero. Orders of vanishing would therefore equate an odd integer with an even one, which is impossible for nonzero entire inputs. This excludes that specific normalization; intrinsic compression to the restricted carrier has separate compatibility equations.

The sign examples use translated smooth bumps with coefficient polynomials that cancel the two moments exactly. At p=11, the finite coefficient sums are -46/11 and (28 sqrt(11)-92)/11, the latter positive. A positive common factor transfers these signs to the smooth local form. Longer packets produce positive values at every prime, while their required supports grow. The sign calculation therefore does not give one fixed test window that works uniformly across all primes.

For an intersection form with the convention I = -B_W, adjoining a relevant prime requires an increment of +W_p. The positive examples rule out an isometric, orthogonal enlargement whose added part is nonpositive on the full moment kernel. Mixed pairings and changing primitive projections remain possible. Sign conditions imposed only after every prime relevant to a test's support has been included also require a different analysis.
