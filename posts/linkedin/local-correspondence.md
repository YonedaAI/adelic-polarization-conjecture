Signed shell maps can respect algebraic boundaries without preserving their original Fourier symmetry. Prime-Adjoining Correspondences studies this tension in a 20-page account of semilocal adelic operators. The local Sonin coefficient has values 1 and -1/p on two shells. Tensoring directly with that coefficient fails to preserve multiplication, so it cannot supply the proposed algebra map.

A signed sum of the maps from the two shell embeddings does give a map of algebraic Hochschild complexes. The boundary commutes with this map, and adjoining two distinct primes gives the same result in either order. In degree zero, it recovers the Sonin coefficient. In every positive degree, the tensor coefficient has an explicit Fourier defect. These are different requirements on the construction, and the signed chain map meets only the first.

The analytic calculation also separates a change of norm from a local arithmetic distribution. Adjoining p multiplies the Fourier-coordinate squared-norm density by |1-p^(-1/2-it)|^2. The logarithmic derivative of the Euler multiplier gives the prime-power distribution in the explicit formula. A raw difference of squared norms cannot be substituted for that distribution. For a compact test, only finitely many prime powers contribute, with an explicit bound determined by its support.

These results give concrete equations for an arithmetic comparison: a chain construction must reproduce the prime-power distribution and address the Fourier defect in positive degree. A primitive geometric pairing still requires a separate construction.
