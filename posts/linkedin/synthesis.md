The restriction cone contains a nonzero relative class, but its compact-bump origin representative becomes a boundary after a single prime is adjoined. An explicit filling proves this for each shell embedding separately, including p=2. This witness therefore cannot supply a surviving class in the prime-indexed direct limit.

Adelic Polarization Conjecture develops the construction in 21 pages. Its source is a semilocal Schwartz crossed product by the arithmetic unit group. Restriction to the invertible locus gives a larger target coefficient algebra. Evaluation at the origin detects a nonzero source class; division by the real coordinate writes its restriction as a commutator. The resulting relative cycle is nonzero at the initial stage. Signed shell maps act on the cone and commute across distinct primes.

The transition proof uses an odd, locally constant function on a new shell and an even compact cutoff in the old variables. A four-term Hochschild chain then fills the remaining target cycle. Together these chains give the full cone boundary. The result concerns this compact-bump witness. The images of other relative classes, and the full direct limit, remain undetermined.

The support-indexed polarization conjecture asks for a primitive arithmetic realization with geometric degree maps and an independently constructed pairing. Its comparison must include the complete local explicit formula on every admissible test window. The classical Weil criterion already applies to the full two-moment kernel; constructing the geometric pairing and proving its sign remain the missing steps. The algebraic cone and its prime maps do not yet provide that polarization.
