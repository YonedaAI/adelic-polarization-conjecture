Two ways of adding a prime can agree on the algebra and disagree on Fourier symmetry.

Prime-Adjoining Correspondences constructs signed maps from two p-adic shells. They respect the boundary operation used to define homology, and distinct primes can be added in either order. But applying Fourier transformation separately to the tensor factors fails to preserve the same coefficient in every positive degree.

There is a second distinction behind the formulas. Adding a prime changes a Sonin space's norm. The prime-power contribution in the arithmetic explicit formula comes from a logarithmic derivative, not from subtracting the old norm from the new one.

The 20-page paper gives the maps and calculations needed to keep those two operations separate. A geometric pairing with the required sign remains a further construction.
