Canceling two unwanted moments does not force a local prime contribution to have one sign.

Obstructions to Adelic Polarization constructs smooth test functions for which the prime-11 contribution is negative, and others for which it is positive. Both examples satisfy the same two moment conditions. Longer constructions give positive examples at every prime, but need larger supports.

This matters when trying to build a geometric pairing one prime at a time. If the new part is orthogonal to the old part and always contributes a nonpositive value, it cannot reproduce those positive local increments.

The 21-page paper also rules out positive pointwise polar normalization of entire functions: a simple zero in an Euler denominator creates an impossible parity equation for orders of vanishing. Other constructions, including ones with mixed pairings, still need their own analysis.
